Fluiddynamicsvideo: Snappingshrimpproduceasnappingsoundbyanextremely
rapid closure of their snapper claw. Our high speed imaging of the claw closure has
revealed that the sound is generated by the collapse of a cavitation bubble formed in
a fast flowing water jet forced out from the claws during claw closure. The produced
sound originates from the cavitation collapse of the bubble. At collapse a short flash
of light is emitted, just as in single bubble sonoluminescence. A model based on the
Rayleigh-Plesset equation can quantitatively account for the visual and acoustical
observations.
The video “On the sound of snapping shrimp” contains various sequences of the animal
itself, the claw closure, the bubble formation and its collapse, and an animatd explantion of
the hydrodynamical model.
